# Nature-style writing, polishing, and paper QA

Use after [writing.md](writing.md) for Nature-style manuscripts, proposals, initial-submission materials, and journal-specific English.

## Evidence before prose

Establish the smallest useful contracts: `scope`, verified `research canon`, `evidence table` (claim → source/result → status), `argument map` (gap → question → contribution → evidence → boundary), and per-section `purpose / inputs / allowed claims / forbidden claims / validation`. Detect paper type, section, language, and target journal; load only the relevant guidance. If evidence or boundaries are missing, write a placeholder or `AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED`, never invent.

## Argument and placement

Make the chain legible: problem/stakes → specific gap → contribution → method/design → evidence → boundary. One paragraph has one job and opens with its point; reverse-outline sections and remove paragraphs that cannot support the argument. Classify results as core discovery, necessary support, conclusion-changing qualification, robustness, heterogeneity, provenance, alternative inference, or edge case. Put the shortest sufficient evidence chain in the main text and move detail to captions, Methods, source data, or Supplementary Information when appropriate.

## Polishing and anti-defensive writing

Structure outranks sentence polish. Apply journal, section, and language conventions last. Use British spelling only when requested or required by the target. Preserve technical terms, equations, citations, numbers, and figure/table references. Remove self-protective padding, staged openings, vague hedges, inflated significance, repeated conclusions, and AI-like formatting. Keep uncertainty that affects validity or interpretation, state limitations once in the proper section, and never turn `may indicate` into `proves` or hide unfavorable findings.

For LaTeX layout, render before and after; inspect pages/contact sheets, then fix floats, spacing, overflow, and sparse pages. Do not judge layout from `.tex` alone. Final QA checks claim support, terminology, cross-section numbers, paragraph necessity, and missing inputs.
