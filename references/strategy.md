# Strategy: ideation and plans

Use for undefined problems, research directions, product concepts, experiments, or multi-step execution.

## Frame before generating

Extract: phase (generate, expand, select, unblock, refine, synthesize), domain, constraints, audience, and decision. A missing constraint is usually more damaging than a missing idea. Ask one question if the phase or target is genuinely ambiguous.

Choose one method that fits the bottleneck. Generate broadly, reject the first obvious answers, then develop the survivors. Do not mistake a named technology for a mechanism.

Every candidate must include:

- a one-line proposition;
- the specific mechanism or causal path;
- why this target user or field needs it now;
- a decisive failure mode or trade-off;
- the smallest real test that could invalidate or advance it.

For research ideas, treat novelty as a claim to verify against literature, not a stylistic label. Compare candidates on importance, tractability, evidence burden, differentiation, and fit with available resources.

## Plan for execution, not ceremony

State goal, non-goals, deliverables, constraints, dependencies, and validation. Sequence by irreversible decisions and evidence risk. Each task should name the relevant files/data, change or output, success criterion, and check.

Use test-first or experiment-first work where failure is cheap and signal is clear. Keep plans concise: provide interfaces, pseudocode, or commands only where they prevent a likely mistake. Do not write a second copy of production code in the plan.

At milestones, stop and compare the observed result with the decision criterion. Re-plan only when evidence changes the scope or approach.
