# Research: arXiv and evidence synthesis

Use for literature discovery, paper assessment, source-backed summaries, citation records, and gap analysis.

## Search and screen

Construct several queries from synonyms, method names, application terms, and exclusions. Search title, abstract, author, category, and exact phrases as needed. Use arXiv for discovery and versioned preprints; use publisher/DOI records for publication status; use citation graphs to find foundational and competing work.

For every retained paper, record title, authors, year, venue/status, DOI or versioned arXiv URL, what question it answers, method/data, principal finding, and a relevant limitation. Check withdrawal notices and preserve the version read. A newer preprint is not automatically a peer-reviewed result.

## Synthesize, do not catalogue

Organize notes around the user's question rather than paper-by-paper summaries:

| Question | Evidence | Agreement/conflict | Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

State whether a claim is directly reported, an inference across sources, or an unresolved hypothesis. Citation counts and venue are discovery signals, not evidence of truth. A research gap is useful only if it identifies a specific missing test, comparison, population, mechanism, or measurement and a feasible way to close it.

Use primary sources for technical claims whenever possible. Do not cite an abstract for a detailed methodological or numerical conclusion without checking the full text.
