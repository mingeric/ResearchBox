# Writing: papers, natural prose, and anti-defensive editing

Use for academic papers, reports, proposals, reviewer responses, summaries, and prose revision.

## Build the argument first

Create a mini-outline: thesis, section role, paragraph claim, evidence, and transition. One paragraph has one job and begins near its point. Reverse-outline completed sections: every paragraph must support the section claim, and every central claim must map to evidence.

For a paper, make the story legible: problem and stakes → specific gap → contribution → method/design → evidence → boundary. Abstracts and introductions should not promise more than results show. Methods explain what was done; experiments answer a defined claim; conclusions reinforce the supported takeaway rather than introducing new claims.

## Claim-evidence discipline

Maintain a compact table for consequential statements:

| Claim | Evidence | Status | Revision needed |
|---|---|---|---|

Use **supported**, **qualified**, or **needs evidence**. Stabilize terminology, quantities, citations, figure/table references, and scope across the document. When evidence is weak, narrow the claim, improve the evidence, or mark the uncertainty; never add a citation or result that is not present.

## Anti-defensive pass

Classify every caveat: necessary scope, methodological limitation, evidence-based qualification, useful contrast, or defensive padding. Delete padding that only anticipates a hypothetical reviewer. Replace negative self-limitation with positive scope. Keep limitations that affect validity, interpretation, safe use, ethics, law, or reproducibility; state them once and where readers need them. Do not hide material negative findings.

Every experiment needs an argumentative duty: the claim tested, evidence supplied, and boundary revealed. Strengthen an argument with better concepts, comparison, evidence, and logic, never with apology or overstatement.

## Humanize without distortion

Preserve facts, numbers, citations, technical literals, links, and the author's voice. Remove staged openings, fake contrasts, repeated one-line conclusions, empty intensifiers, sales language, decorative headings/bold, excessive dashes, forced triads, vague associations, chatbot wrappers, and unsupported authority. Vary sentence rhythm only where it does not reduce clarity. Keep real qualifications and deliberate stylistic choices.
