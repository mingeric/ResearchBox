# Slides: PowerPoint and academic presentations

Use whenever a `.pptx`, deck, slide template, speaker notes, or presentation narrative is involved.

## Narrative and system

Build a talk rather than a document cut into slides: question → evidence → interpretation → decision/next step. Give each slide one message, and use the clearest visual form for it: figure, comparison, process, timeline, or large number. Do not use text-only bullets where a diagram, figure, or table would reveal the relationship.

Inspect an existing deck's layouts, fonts, colors, placeholders, notes, and content before editing. For a new deck, choose a topic-specific palette with one dominant color, a limited support palette, a type hierarchy, and a repeated visual motif. Vary layouts because messages differ; preserve grid, margins, contrast, and spacing.

## Content and rendering QA

Keep titles and labels large enough for presentation viewing, align body text left unless centering is intentional, and keep sources readable. Avoid fabricated statistics, filler, low-contrast elements, title-underlines used as decoration, and leftover template text.

After generation or editing, extract text to check order and omissions, then render slides to images/PDF and inspect them critically. Look for overflow, clipping, overlap, awkward wrapping, collisions with citations/footers, inconsistent alignment, uneven gaps, inaccessible contrast, and placeholders. Fix and re-render affected slides; do not call a deck complete before a full visual pass.
